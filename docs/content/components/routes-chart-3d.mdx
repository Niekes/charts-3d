The routes chart joins the entries of `data` over the globe: every entry is
a stop at its `lon` and `lat`, and a leg runs from each stop to the next
along the great circle, the shortest way over the surface. `series` names
the field that tells the routes apart; without it all the entries are one
route. What lies on the far side is hidden by the globe, so a leg ends at
the horizon. `id` names a stop, in the tooltip and through updates. Pointing
at a route lights the whole route: a leg says its two stops, a stop itself.
An entry whose `lon` or `lat` is missing or not a number is no stop, and its
route ends before it and starts again after it.

The demos travel between the capitals of
[Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/),
public domain. The `land` under the routes is the one every globe takes, cut
at the horizon by d3-geo; see the [globe](/docs/components/globe-chart-3d)
for where it comes from and how its regions must be wound. For places
without a route between them, see the [places chart](/docs/components/places-chart-3d).

## Enter and leave

A stop that joins the end of its route grows its leg out of the stop before
it, along the great circle, as it fades in; a stop that goes draws its leg
back into its neighbour, the mirror of its entering (see Enter in the
[glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).
Here a tour shows the stop it left and the stop it heads for: with every
step the leg behind draws back towards the stop it led to as the next one
grows. The globe turns to the new stop through the `view` of the container,
set to `facing(lon, lat)`: a heading, so you can turn the globe away at any
time (see [chart-3d](/docs/components/chart-3d)).

## Many marks

Every place of half a million people and more on one route, each time on to
the nearest one not yet visited: a thousand stops and the pieces of a
thousand legs, nearly three thousand marks - and the chart stays interactive.
The scene is one list of marks, so the demo starts on `"canvas"`, the
renderer for many marks, and the select shows the same scene on SVG.
