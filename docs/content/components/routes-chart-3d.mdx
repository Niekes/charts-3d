The routes chart joins the entries of `data` over the globe: every entry is
a stop at its `lon` and `lat`, and a leg runs from each stop to the next
along the great circle, the shortest way over the surface. `series` names
the field that tells the routes apart; without it all the entries are one
route. What lies on the far side is hidden by the globe, so a leg ends at
the horizon. `id` names a stop, in the tooltip and through updates; a route
may come back to a stop, since a name that repeats is a stop of its own. Pointing
at a leg lights that leg alone and says its two stops; a stop lights and
says itself.
An entry whose `lon` or `lat` is missing or not a number is no stop, and its
route ends before it and starts again after it.

A route has the color of its series. `leg` names a field that gives a leg a
color of its own: its value, at the stop a leg arrives at, is the config key
of that leg - how it was travelled, here by air or by sea. The leg and that
stop take the color, the first stop of a route takes that of the leg that
leaves it, and the tooltip names it in its title. A stop that names no leg,
and one no leg touches, keeps the color of its route. With a leg named at
every stop, as here, the config needs the keys of the legs only: `series`
still tells the routes apart.

## The tip

The last stop of a route is its tip: where the route stands now. A config
key may carry a `symbol`, and the tip is drawn as the symbol of the key that
colors it - its leg, or else its route - in place of the circle; every stop
before it stays a circle, and a key without a symbol keeps the circle on the
tip too. Here the route round the Atlantic comes home by air and ends on a
plane; the one to the east ends by sea, on a ship off Canberra - turn the
globe to see it.

```tsx
const chartConfig = {
  air: {
    label: "By air",
    color: "var(--chart-1)",
    symbol: { path: "M17.8 19.2 16 11...", points: 45, fill: true },
  },
  sea: { label: "By sea", color: "var(--chart-2)", symbol: { path: "M12 2v2 M12 9.189V13..." } },
} satisfies ChartConfig
```

A symbol is the outline of an icon as path data, in a box of 24: the `d` of
its `<path>`, as [Lucide](https://lucide.dev), Tabler, Heroicons or Phosphor
write it, the `d`s of several paths joined by a space. The registry brings no
icon package: copy the path from your set. An icon that draws with a
`<circle>` or a `<rect>`, as the wheels of Lucide's car, has to become a path
first, which [SVGO](https://svgo.dev/docs/plugins/convertShapeToPath/) does.
It is drawn in the one color of the tip, as an outline, the way Lucide draws
its icons, or filled with `fill: true`, as the icons of a solid set; ringed
by the background, as large on the screen from every side, and it lights on
hover as a stop does.

`points` says where the symbol points as it is drawn, in degrees clockwise
from up: 45 for the plane of Lucide. A symbol that names it turns to its
bearing - the leg that arrives at the tip, as the camera shows it - so the
plane points the way it flew. One that names none stays upright, as a ship or
a car seen from the side. A tip without a leg points the way it moves - a
route of one stop whose place changes is a vehicle on its way - and where it
stands it keeps the bearing it came with, as one whose leg has drawn back
into it on a tour that drops the stops behind it. A tip that never came from
anywhere shows its symbol as it is drawn.

The tip moves with the data. A stop that joins the end of a route takes the
symbol at once and flies its leg from the stop it stood on, whole all the
way; one that leaves the data draws back along its leg as it fades, and the
stop before it is the tip once it is gone. See it fly under
[Enter and leave](#enter-and-leave). The chart draws no flag: a flag has
many colors, and belongs in the text beside a stop.

The demos travel between the capitals of
[Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/),
public domain. The `land` under the routes is the one every globe takes, cut
at the horizon by d3-geo; see the [globe](/docs/components/globe-chart-3d)
for where it comes from and how its regions must be wound. For places
without a route between them, see the [places chart](/docs/components/places-chart-3d).

## Enter and leave

A stop that joins the end of its route grows its leg out of the stop before
it, along the great circle, as it fades in; a stop that goes draws its leg
back into its neighbour, the mirror of its entering (see Enter in the
[glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).
Here a tour rests on one stop. A step adds the next stop, so its leg grows
out of the stop the tour is on - the plane, the symbol of the route, flies
it - and then drops the stop left behind, so the
leg draws back towards the new one. The globe turns to the new stop through
the `view` of the container, set to `facing(lon, lat)`: a heading, so you can
turn the globe away at any time (see [chart-3d](/docs/components/chart-3d#view)).
Steps may come faster than they end: every stop goes on from where it is,
a new one starting on the stop before it wherever that has come to, and
each step drops only what lies behind the stop it added.

## Many marks

Every place of half a million people and more on one route, each time on to
the nearest one not yet visited: a thousand stops and the pieces of a
thousand legs, nearly three thousand marks - and the chart stays interactive.
The scene is one list of marks, so the demo starts on `"canvas"`, the
renderer for many marks, and the select shows the same scene on SVG.
