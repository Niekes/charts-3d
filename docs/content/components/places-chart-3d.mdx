The places chart puts a dot on the globe for every entry, at its `lon` and
`lat`, in the color of its `series`. With a `value`, the area of a dot is its
share of the largest value, so a place of four times the people gets a dot of
twice the radius; without one, every dot is as large as `radius`. A dot on the
far side is hidden by the globe. `id` names an entry: the name is the title of
its tooltip and its identity through updates, so a dot follows its name when
the data changes. Without `id`, the tooltip shows the series and the place.

The demos show the populated places of
[Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/),
public domain, with the people of their metro areas, a capital in its own
color. The `land` under the dots is the one every globe takes, cut at the
horizon by d3-geo; see the [globe](/docs/components/globe-chart-3d) for where
it comes from and how its regions must be wound. The dots are few, so the
demos start on `"svg"`, and the many demo on `"canvas"`.

## Update

Press the button: every dot glides from the people of its metro area to the
people of its city proper, and back, over the `animationDuration` of its
container. The largest value glides along, so no dot jumps.

## Enter and leave

New data may also bring and drop places. A place that arrives grows out of
the globe from nothing as it fades in; one that goes shrinks back and fades
out, the mirror of its entering (see Enter in the
[glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).
Here the bar moves from the megacities down to every place.

## Renderers

The same chart on every renderer, side by side. The marks are data the chart hands to the container, and `"svg"`, `"canvas"` and `"webgl"` draw them all - where the browser has the requested renderer, `"webgl"` falling back to `"canvas"`, and where it has none, to SVG.

## Many marks

Every place of Natural Earth, seven thousand dots over the land, the smallest
a speck - and the chart stays interactive. The scene is one list of marks, so
the demo starts on `"canvas"`, the renderer for many marks, and the select
shows the same scene on SVG and WebGL.
