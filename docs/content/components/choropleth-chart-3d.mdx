The choropleth colors the regions of the land on a globe. An entry names a
region by the `id` of its feature - in [world-atlas](https://github.com/topojson/world-atlas)
the ISO 3166-1 numeric code, `"276"` for Germany - and the region takes the
color of the entry's `series`, a config key per class; the `value` shows in
its tooltip, titled by the `label` of the entry or the label of its series. A
region without an entry stays land, and the last entry on a region wins.
Nothing grows here: a region fades in with its entry and out without it, and
one that changes its series leaves as one and enters as the other, since
motion would mislead on a map.

The demos show the countries of the world from the admin 0 file of
[Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/110m-cultural-vectors/)
(public domain), each in the fifth of the countries its people fall in, in
five steps of YlOrRd; France and Norway carry no ISO numeric code there and
get theirs in `docs/examples/countries.ts`. The classes and the fifths live in
`docs/examples/regions.ts`, not in the chart. The `land` is the one the
[globe](/docs/components/globe-chart-3d) takes, with the same winding, and it
turns, pans and zooms with the regions. A few hundred rings draw fine on SVG,
so the demos start there.

## Update

Press the button: the countries are classed by their GDP instead of their
people, and every country that changes its fifth fades from the one color to
the other, over the `animationDuration` of its container.

## Enter and leave

New data may also bring and drop regions. A country that arrives fades in
over the land; one that goes fades back to it, the mirror of its entering (see
Enter in the [glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).
Here the world arrives country by country, from the ones of a hundred million
people down to every country; the fifths are taken among the countries shown,
so a country may change its color as others join.

## Renderers

The same chart on every renderer, side by side. The marks are data the chart hands to the container, and `"svg"`, `"canvas"` and `"webgl"` draw them all - where the browser has the requested renderer, `"webgl"` falling back to `"canvas"`, and where it has none, to SVG.

## Many marks

Every country colored, on `"canvas"`, the renderer for many marks, with the
select for the same scene on SVG and WebGL. The size of a choropleth is its
land's, not its data's: the 177 regions of world-atlas at 110m are 10,600
points, which d3-geo cuts at the horizon on every turn of the camera, and a
colored region is drawn once, in its color alone. At 50m the regions are
100,000 points, and the cut alone took 100 ms a frame on a laptop, so the
demos stay at 110m; a finer land is where the budget of this chart ends.
