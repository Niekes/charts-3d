## Renderers

The marks of a chart - its bars, points and faces - are drawn by the renderer of the container. `"svg"`, the default, draws them as SVG elements; `"canvas"` paints them on a canvas, for charts with many marks; `"webgl"` triangulates them on the GPU, for charts with the most.

```tsx
<ChartContainer3D config={chartConfig} renderer="webgl">
```

The box, its labels, the tooltip, the legend and the gizmo stay SVG and HTML with every renderer. A chart draws as SVG on the server and until the browser shows it has the requested renderer, `"webgl"` falling back to `"canvas"`, and stays SVG where it has none.

## Shortcuts

Press `?` on a chart, or the keyboard button in its corner, for the shortcuts that rotate, pan, zoom and reset it; Escape or a click outside closes the panel again. Each row names its action, then lists its keys and - past a thin rule - its pointer gestures, with the drag written out as "Drag". The platform modifier and a drag rotate - ⌘ on a Mac, Ctrl on Windows and Linux; a plain drag only inspects, with the default cursor - Alt (⌥ on a Mac) and a drag pans, the platform modifier with the wheel zooms to the cursor - a trackpad pinch arrives as Ctrl and the wheel, so it zooms on every platform - and a double click resets; the wheel alone scrolls the page. The cursor names the gesture: a hand while the platform modifier is held, `grabbing` while the drag turns, the four arrows while Alt is held, `zoom-in` or `zoom-out` while the wheel zooms. See [ADR-0001](../adr/0001-navigation). `shortcuts={false}` hides the button. The chart announces its keys to screen readers through `aria-keyshortcuts`, and the keys in the panel carry `data-slot="kbd"`, as the Kbd of shadcn/ui does, so `className="[&_[data-slot=kbd]]:bg-background"` on the container restyles them. The button is `data-slot="chart-3d-shortcuts-trigger"`, the list `data-slot="chart-3d-shortcuts"`.

### Keymap

`keymap` rebinds, renames or turns off every action, over its defaults. Keep it outside the component, so it stays the same object between renders.

```tsx
const keymap: Keymap3D = {
  rotateLeft: ["a", "ArrowLeft"], // new keys; the panel shows the first
  zoomIn: { keys: ["Control+Plus"], label: "Vergrößern" },
  reset: false, // the action is off
  wheelZoom: false, // so is the gesture, and the modifier with the wheel zooms the page
  help: { label: "Tastenkürzel" }, // the name of the button and of its row
}

<ChartContainer3D config={chartConfig} keymap={keymap}>
```

`keymap={false}` turns everything off: the chart stands still, leaves the tab order and shows no button. The panel lists the actions that are on, one row per label, so actions that share a label share a row.

| Action             | Default keys         | Label              |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------ |
| `dragRotate`       | ⌘/Ctrl and drag      | Rotate             |
| `rotateLeft`       | `ArrowLeft`          | Rotate             |
| `rotateRight`      | `ArrowRight`         | Rotate             |
| `rotateUp`         | `ArrowUp`            | Rotate             |
| `rotateDown`       | `ArrowDown`          | Rotate             |
| `dragPan`          | Alt/⌥ and drag       | Pan                |
| `panLeft`          | `Shift+ArrowLeft`    | Pan                |
| `panRight`         | `Shift+ArrowRight`   | Pan                |
| `panUp`            | `Shift+ArrowUp`      | Pan                |
| `panDown`          | `Shift+ArrowDown`    | Pan                |
| `zoomIn`           | `Plus`, `=`          | Zoom               |
| `zoomOut`          | `-`                  | Zoom               |
| `wheelZoom`        | ⌘/Ctrl and the wheel | Zoom               |
| `reset`            | `0`                  | Reset              |
| `doubleClickReset` | Double-click         | Reset              |
| `help`             | `?`                  | Keyboard shortcuts |

Keys are written the way `aria-keyshortcuts` writes them: the modifiers `Control`, `Alt`, `Meta` and `Shift`, then the [`key`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/KeyboardEvent/key) of the press, joined by `+`, with `Plus` for the plus key and `Space` for the space bar. A press matches only with exactly the modifiers of its keys, so Ctrl and `0` still resets the zoom of the browser. Shift counts beside a named key such as `ArrowLeft`; a character such as `?` is typed with it already, so `"?"` matches without it. The gestures can be turned off and renamed, but not rebound.

The keys work only while the chart has the focus, and every one can be turned off or rebound, as [WCAG 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html) asks of shortcuts with a single character.

## Gizmo

The axes in the lower left corner turn with the camera, so a turned chart still shows which way is up and where its axes point. They neither pan nor zoom. `gizmo` moves them to another corner, `gizmo={false}` hides them.

```tsx
<ChartContainer3D config={chartConfig} gizmo="top-left">
```

The gizmo is `data-slot="chart-3d-gizmo"`, drawn in `--muted-foreground` and hidden from screen readers. Each axis draws in `currentColor` and carries `data-axis`, so `className="[&_[data-axis=x]]:text-red-500"` on the container colors one axis.

## Tooltip

The tooltip of a mark follows the pointer, above and to its right. Where it would leave the chart it flips to the other side - on each axis alone, and only to a side with more room - and glides there; where the reader asks for reduced motion, it jumps. `tooltip` puts it in another corner of the pointer ([ADR-0005](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/docs/adr/0005-tooltip-follows-pointer.md)).

```tsx
<ChartContainer3D config={chartConfig} tooltip="bottom-left">
```

The tooltip is `data-slot="chart-3d-tooltip"`, and its `data-placement` names the corner it took, flipped or not - `"top-left"`, `"top-right"`, `"bottom-left"` or `"bottom-right"` - so CSS can style each corner. To square the corner that points at the pointer:

```tsx
<ChartContainer3D
  config={chartConfig}
  className="[&_[data-placement=top-right]]:rounded-bl-none [&_[data-placement=top-left]]:rounded-br-none [&_[data-placement=bottom-right]]:rounded-tl-none [&_[data-placement=bottom-left]]:rounded-tr-none"
>
```

What a mark says is built from the data behind it, its values as the ticks name them through `format`, until `tooltip` on the chart says otherwise: a function of what the mark would say - a title and its items, a label and a value each - and of the data behind the mark, in the words of the chart's props: the `entry` and series `key` of a bar, the `entry` of a point, the `key` and the spanned `entries` of a face of a ribbon, the `row`, `column` and `value` of a cell. It runs when the pointer lands on the mark, and `null` shows none ([ADR-0006](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/docs/adr/0006-tooltip-content.md)).

```tsx
<BarChart3D
  data={data}
  index="month"
  tooltip={(tooltip, { entry, key }) => ({
    ...tooltip,
    title: `${entry.month} · ${key}`,
    items: tooltip.items.map((item) => ({ ...item, value: `${entry[key]} €` })),
  })}
/>
```

## Focus

A mark can name the groups it belongs to in `groups`. Pointing at a mark lights the fill of every mark that shares one of its groups - the faces of a bar, the segments of a flow - in the focus color: `--chart-3d-focus`, or its own fill lightened (see [Theming](/docs/theming)). A stroke of the mark's own stays, and nothing else changes, so the order the marks paint in, and with it the depth, stays as it was, on the canvas too. Off the mark or off the chart the focus clears; through a drag that turns the chart it stays, so what is in focus can be followed from every side. Marks without groups never light.

```tsx
// The marks of a flow share its key; the marks of a node carry the keys of all its flows.
const marks = cubeMarks(key, faces, color, tooltip).map((mark) => ({ ...mark, groups: [flow.key] }))
```

## Labels

The labels of the box sit beside their ticks, away from the box, and slide round them as the camera turns. Where two would touch, they slide apart along their axis, and where that is not enough, every other one moves to a second row; only an axis seen nearly end on shows every second, fourth… label. A label gives way to one of an axis with fewer ticks, whose labels say more each. When an axis moves to another edge of the box, its labels fade from one edge to the other.

The arrow keys glide the camera to its next view instead of jumping there. Where the reader asks for reduced motion, the camera jumps and the labels move without a transition.

## Transitions

A chart glides into new data over the `animationDuration` of its container; 0 makes it jump, and so does a reader who asks for reduced motion. The glide happens in the numbers, before the projection - `useTween` eases them from the ones on screen - so the camera answers at once, mid-glide too. Every key moves as its own mark ([Enter](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md) in the glossary):

- A key the data **brings** starts at its enter value: a new bar grows out of the floor, at its place, and fades in as it grows.
- A key that **stays** glides from where it stands to its new number - also to a new place, when the data reorders.
- A key the data **drops** glides back to its enter value, the mirror of its entering, and leaves when the glide is done: a removed bar shrinks back into the floor and fades out as it shrinks, so nothing flat pops away at the end.

A mark fades with `faded(color, opacity)`, a `color-mix` with transparent that every renderer draws: tween an opacity per key with `useTween` - 1, with 0 as the enter value - and fade the color after `shade`.

The axes glide along (`useTweenAxes`): their ends ease instead of jumping from one round number to the next, a tick that stays glides as the same label, an entering one fades in, and a dropped one fades out - in place, or as the gliding end sweeps past it - while the line of the axis grows to a new outermost tick and shrinks from a dropped one. The [bar chart's Update and Enter-and-leave demos](/docs/components/bar-chart-3d#update) show all of it; a chart of your own gets the same behaviour by tweening its numbers with `useTween` and its axes with `useTweenAxes` before it projects.
