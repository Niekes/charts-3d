The globe is a grid of tiles between meridians and parallels, `step` degrees
wide, on the land. An entry names a place, in the `lon` and `lat` fields, and
the tile that holds it rises to the entry's `value`, in the color of its
`series`; a tile without an entry is not drawn. Aggregation happens before
the chart: round a list of places to the grid, or hand the chart a grid it
already has, one entry per tile - where two entries fall on one tile, the
last one wins. The tile is the identity of a mark: an entry that moves to
another tile leaves there and enters here, and a tile that changes its series
leaves as one and enters as the other. `label` names a field whose value, of
the last entry on a tile, titles its tooltip. For regions colored by a value
see the [choropleth](/docs/components/choropleth-chart-3d), for a dot at
every place the [places chart](/docs/components/places-chart-3d): the three
share the land.

The demos show the populated places of the world, the 7,332 cities and towns
of [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/)
with the people of their metro areas, summed on tiles of 2°: a tile rises to
the people on it, and its color is their class, nine steps of the square root
up to 10 million in YlOrRd, as the
[world population of globe.gl](https://globe.gl/example/world-population/)
colors its hexagons; the `legend` of the container lists the classes. Most
tiles are flat sparks, a few dozen are towers. The classes and the sums live
in `docs/examples/population.ts`, not in the chart: the chart takes a
`series` per tile and a config entry per class, as any chart does. Thousands
of tiles are too many for SVG while the camera turns, so every demo starts on
`"canvas"`.

The `land` is a GeoJSON feature collection, the regions of the earth: d3-geo
cuts them at the horizon and lays them on the ocean disc, flat in the colors
of the walls - or in `--chart-3d-ocean` and `--chart-3d-land` where you set
them, as these docs do for a globe at night in the dark theme, see
[Theming](/docs/theming#the-chart-tokens) - and they turn, pan and zoom with
the tiles. The land is scenery: it never enters, glides or answers the
pointer. The demos take the land of
[world-atlas](https://github.com/topojson/world-atlas) at 110m, its
`countries`, converted from TopoJSON with
[topojson-client](https://github.com/topojson/topojson-client),
as `docs/examples/land.ts` shows; the chart takes GeoJSON alone. Mind the
winding: d3-geo reads a region wound clockwise, the opposite of RFC 7946, and
a region wound the other way fills the globe around it - `topojson-client`
winds them right, a GeoJSON file from elsewhere may not. The globe is
orthographic, as d3-geo projects it, so it ignores the `perspective` of the
camera.

## Update

Press the button: every tile glides from the people of its metro areas to
the people of its cities proper, and back, over the `animationDuration` of
its container, and the ceiling - the height of the tallest tile - glides
along, so no tile jumps. The class of a tile stays that of its metro areas,
so every tile glides and none leaves.

## Enter and leave

New data may also bring and drop tiles. A quake that arrives grows out of the
globe and fades in; one that goes shrinks back into it and fades out, the
mirror of its entering (see Enter in the
[glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).
Here the world arrives place by place: lowering the bar from the megacities
to every place, the smaller places enter and light up; raising it, they sink
back. A tile that was there already grows as a place joins it, and leaves as
one class to enter as another where the newcomers lift it over a bound.

## Many marks

Tiles of 1°, nearly every place its own spark, five thousand tiles over the
land - and the chart stays interactive. A tile thinner than a pixel draws its
top alone, so the globe projects a face per flat tile, not five. The scene is
one list of marks, so the demo starts on `"canvas"`, the renderer for many
marks, and the select shows the same scene on SVG.
