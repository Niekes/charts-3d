Every series is the area under its line, from the floor of the box, pushed
back into a ribbon: the entries run along x, one ribbon per series along z.
Pointing at a face highlights that face alone; a face of the top tells the values
at its two ends.

## Update

Press Update: the ribbons morph into new prices over the `animationDuration`
of their container, and the y axis scales along. The chart glides the prices,
not the faces, so every frame is projected, culled and ordered anew.

## Enter and leave

New data may also bring, drop and reorder whole entries - a point follows the
name of its entry, in the `index` field. An added year unfolds from the year
before it, so the ribbons grow out of their ends while the x axis makes room,
its line growing to the new tick, its label fading in; a removed one folds
back into the year before it, the mirror of its entering, while the axis
closes the gap (see Enter in the
[glossary](https://github.com/Niekes/charts-3d/blob/master/GLOSSARY.md)).

## Renderers

The same chart on every renderer, side by side. The marks are data the chart hands to the container, and `"svg"`, `"canvas"` and `"webgl"` draw them all - where the browser has the requested renderer, `"webgl"` falling back to `"canvas"`, and where it has none, to SVG. The front of a ribbon is one concave polygon, which WebGL cuts into triangles ear by ear.

## Many marks

Twelve sensors over two hundred days - about 2,400 faces on the tops of their
ribbons - and the chart stays interactive. The scene is one list of marks, so the demo starts on
`"canvas"`, the renderer for many marks, and the select shows the same scene on
SVG and WebGL.
